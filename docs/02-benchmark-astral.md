# Trace benchmark — ProteoBench Astral entrapment (2026-10-09)

DIATraceExtractor vs DIA-NN 1.7.12 (last open-source release) vs OpenSWATH (OpenMS develop, 3.7.0-pre)
on the three ProteoBench DIA Astral entrapment runs (`LFQ_Astral_DIA_15min_50ng_Human_01..03`, 15-min
gradient, 300 × 2 Th windows). Reproduce with `scripts/benchmark_run.sh <run>`.

## Setup

- **Library**: DIALibGen 0.11.0 (AlphaPeptDeep ONNX, Astral → Lumos model, NCE 25), UniProt human
  reference proteome UP000005640, Trypsin/P 1 MC, 7–30 aa, z 1–4, CAM fixed, Ox(M) variable —
  3,488,457 precursors (`library/human_UP000005640_astral_nce25.parquet`; DIA-NN TSV via
  `scripts/lib_to_diann17_tsv.py`).
- **DIA-NN 1.7.12** searches the full library per run (decoys generated by DIA-NN) and exports XICs
  (`--xic 60`, 60 points around the apex). Its `--xic` export has three defects; two are fixed in
  `tools/DiaNN-1.7.12/diann-1.7.12-xicfix` (source comments `// odia:`), the third is handled by the
  reader (`scripts/diann17_xic_to_parquet.py`): (1) the buffer was sized before the fragment list was
  known → heap overflow and abort; (2) the MS1 intensity block was overwritten by the MS2 RTs; (3) the
  stored group addresses ignore the per-group element count. Identification is unaffected.
- **Recalibration**: RT anchors = (library RT, DIA-NN apex RT) of DIA-NN's 1% FDR identifications, fitted
  with LOWESS in DIATraceExtractor. Systematic mass offsets measured per run by maximising captured
  intensity at ±2 ppm over offsets −5…+3 ppm (5,000 identified precursors).
- **Coordinates**: the same precursors (DIA-NN's 1% FDR IDs), all library fragments, ±60 s around the
  recalibrated RT, ± DIA-NN's optimised mass accuracy. OpenSWATH gets a library generated from the
  extractor's own trace table (identical RT centres/fragments; identity RT transformation, iRT
  calibration off, `-rt_extraction_window 120`, full-width m/z window = 2 × half width).
- **Two extractor configurations**: **A** matched to DIA-NN (max aggregation, mass offsets applied);
  **B** matched to OpenSWATH (sum aggregation, no mass correction, OpenSWATH's isotope spacing).
- **Comparison** (`scripts/compare_traces.py`): join on (precursor, MS level, fragment annotation);
  values compared at the extractor's true acquisition times (MS2) / global cycle axis (MS1), the other
  tool interpolated there; Pearson r, L1 of sum-normalised traces, log2 ratio of sums, apex Δt.

Reading:
- With matched rules the extractor reproduces DIA-NN's fragment traces exactly (median r = 1.000000;
  on run 01 the per-precursor median r is ≥ 0.9 for 99.97% of precursors) and OpenSWATH's to the precision of
  OpenSWATH's lossy numpress/slof `.xic` encoding (r 0.99988, L1 0.02).
- The residual non-agreeing MS2 traces (~5% with r < 0.9 vs DIA-NN) are sparse, noise-level fragments
  whose few points sit at the tolerance edge: DIA-NN's mass correction is m/z- and RT-dependent, ours a
  constant per-run offset. 6% of traces are all-zero in one tool (r undefined).
- MS1 vs DIA-NN is slightly lower because DIA-NN extracts MS1 at the *uncorrected* precursor m/z
  (`ms1_XIC` queries `mz`, not `query_mz`) and both interpolate MS1 differently; vs OpenSWATH (same
  rules) MS1 agrees to 0.9998.
- DIA-NN and OpenSWATH differ from each other mainly through their rules (max vs sum, mass correction),
  r 0.97–0.997 — the extractor can produce either.

Of the extractor's ~51 s, ~14 s is library load and ~34 s the serial mzML XML parse; extraction itself
is ~2 s for 84 K precursors and 46 s (writer-bound) for the full library. The run grid holds the whole
run (141 M peaks) in 1.08 GiB. Not like-for-like with DIA-NN/OpenSWATH (they also score), but they bound
the cost of the extraction stage: the project brief's targets (< 1 h and < 64 GB per run) leave ample room
for everything an integrated tool adds on top.

## Notes

- OpenSWATH places MS1 isotope k at `mz + k·1.00336` regardless of charge
  (ChromatogramExtractor.cpp:161-170); the extractor's default divides by z (physically correct, as
  DIA-NN); `-ms1_isotope_spacing unit` reproduces OpenSWATH (configuration B).
- The ProteoBench entrapment library (`library/proteobench_entrapment_astral_nce25.parquet`, 7.0 M
  precursors) is built but not used here: this benchmark compares traces, not FDR.
- Raw per-trace metrics: `results/compare/runNN/cmpA.per_trace.tsv`, `cmpB.per_trace.tsv`.

## Self-calibration (no DIA-NN input) — `-rt_mode auto -mass_calibration auto`

The extractor calibrates RT and m/z from the run itself (src/Calibration.cpp): a 200,000-precursor panel
stratified over library RT is extracted over the whole run at ±20 ppm; per precursor the best of the 5
largest maxima of the summed fragment trace is accepted when its fragments co-elute (stage 1: ≥ 5
fragments, mean pairwise r ≥ 0.9 over ±4 cycles, cosine of √intensities vs library ≥ 0.85). A robust
initial curve takes the run-RT *mode* per library-RT bin (true anchors cluster, false ones spread), a
LOWESS fit with 3σ rejection follows; stage 2 re-examines the panel within ±max(4σ, 30 s) of that curve
with relaxed criteria (≥ 4 fragments, r ≥ 0.8, cosine ≥ 0.75) and refits. Mass offsets are the
intensity-weighted median ppm errors of the matched centroids at the used anchors. Heuristic calibration,
no classifier or FDR. Cost: ~2–3 s per run on the grid. The panel is always drawn from the whole library
(never from an `-ids` subset).

External check against DIA-NN 1.7.12's 1% FDR apexes (`scripts/validate_calibration.py`):

| run | anchors used | DIA-NN IDs among anchors | median \|ΔRT\| | p95 | within ±30 s | within ±60 s (self) | within ±60 s (map fitted on DIA-NN's 84 K IDs) | MS2 / MS1 offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | 2,006 | 67% | 15.6 s | 112 s | 75.4% | **90.9%** | 89.2% | −2.79 / +1.25 ppm |
| 02 | 1,984 | 69% | 15.5 s | 100 s | 76.0% | **91.5%** | 89.5% | −2.00 / +1.14 ppm |
| 03 | 1,923 | 70% | 15.7 s | 94 s | 75.7% | **91.4%** | 89.0% | −1.62 / +1.31 ppm |

- The self-calibrated map is as good as a LOWESS map fitted on all of DIA-NN's identifications (slightly
  better at ±60 s). The remaining spread is the **predicted library RT's per-peptide error**, which no
  smooth map removes (DIA-NN itself searched ±3.9 min on this library). Closing it needs a refined library
  (per-peptide RT correction, e.g. DIALibGen `tune`/`refine` on run anchors), not a better map.
- Self-calibrated MS2 offsets agree with the intensity scans (−2.5 / −1.5 / −1.5 ppm); MS1 (σ ≈ 0.47 ppm)
  is resolved better by the per-peak estimate than by the ±2 ppm scan.
- Traces with self-calibration vs DIA-NN on run 01: MS2 median r = 1.000000 (91.5% of traces r > 0.9),
  MS1 0.9986 — unchanged from the DIA-NN-anchored configuration.
- Full library (3.49 M precursors), self-calibrated: 98.7 s, 3.2 GiB peak RSS on run 01.

## Shared recalibrated library (all three tools on one DIALibGen library) — `scripts/benchmark_refined.sh`

Per run: DIA-NN 1.7.12 searches the predicted DIALibGen human library (round 1); `DIALibGen -mode refine`
writes the observed RT of its 1% FDR identifications into the library (`-q_global 1 -q_protein 1`; the 1.7.12
report is converted to Parquet with a `Decoy = 0` column, since 1.7.12 reports targets only); that refined
library (RT in observed run minutes) is the single input of all three tools:
DIA-NN 1.7.12 round 2 (`--xic 60`), DIATraceExtractor (`-rt_mode minutes`, ±60 s, all fragments,
tolerance = DIA-NN's optimised mass accuracy), OpenSwathWorkflow (RT in seconds, identity, `-rt_extraction_window 120`,
full width = 2 × tolerance). Extractor A = DIA-NN rules (max, mass offsets measured from the run via
`-mass_calibration auto`); B = OpenSWATH rules (sum, no correction, unit isotope spacing).

reproduces OpenSWATH's MS1 traces to 0.9998.
